# Draft Charter for International AGI Research Cooperation

This is a discussion draft, not a treaty or legal agreement. It combines useful
institutional ideas from large shared-science facilities and international
scientific cooperation. Legal, diplomatic, security, human-rights, competition,
export-control, and intellectual-property experts must review any operational
version.

## Purpose

Create a neutral, peaceful framework in which states, universities, nonprofits,
companies, and independent researchers can contribute to and inspect carefully
bounded AGI research without giving one participant unilateral control.

## Founding principles

1. Peaceful scientific purposes only.
2. Material scientific observations, evaluation methods, incidents, and
   negative results are shared to the greatest safe and lawful extent.
3. Facilities and safety controls are subject to agreed independent inspection.
4. Background intellectual property remains identifiable; jointly produced
   project IP follows pre-agreed access and dissemination rules.
5. Contribution size may affect facility access but must not purchase unilateral
   authority over safety gates or AGI declarations.
6. High-capability artefacts may require controlled access even when evaluation
   results and governance records are open.
7. Withdrawal, suspension, dispute, incident, and emergency shutdown procedures
   are agreed before operation.

The peaceful-use, scientific-exchange, and inspection ideas are inspired by the
[Antarctic Treaty](https://www.ats.aq/e/antarctictreaty.html?lang=en). Shared
contribution and project-IP concepts are informed by the
[ITER framework](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/framework-sharing-iter-intellectual-property).

## Proposed institutional layers

### 1. Open scientific network

Any qualifying researcher can contribute benchmarks, failure reports,
replications, or governance proposals. This layer creates the common evidence
base and does not receive operational model access by default.

### 2. Research consortium

Members sign common evaluation, publication, incident, and conflict-of-interest
rules. Working groups cover architecture, evaluation, hardware, safety,
governance, and societal impact.

### 3. Joint facility organization

A separate legal entity owns or operates shared compute and secure laboratories.
It maintains isolated partitions so builders, evaluators, and inspectors do not
need identical access.

### 4. Independent oversight council

Representation should include technical safety, cybersecurity, law, human
rights, labour, environmental impact, public-interest research, participating
states, and communities likely to bear deployment risks.

## Decision classes

| Class | Example | Minimum approval concept |
|---|---|---|
| Routine science | Benchmark run inside an existing sandbox | Workstream approval and logged review |
| Material capability change | Larger model, new memory, broader tools | Technical + safety boards and independent evaluator |
| High-risk scope change | External network, autonomous execution, sensitive domain | Supermajority plus oversight council and affected-party review |
| AGI claim or containment change | Declare candidate AGI, widen access, remove isolation | Independent replication and multinational governance decision |
| Emergency | Active containment or security incident | Authorized rapid shutdown followed by mandatory review |

No participant can trade financial contribution for the right to bypass a
decision class.

## Contributions

Members may contribute:

- hardware, facilities, energy, and operations;
- models, data, evaluation sets, and software;
- research staff and independent evaluators;
- safety, security, legal, and governance expertise;
- direct funding or in-kind access.

All contributions require a valuation method, declared conditions, provenance,
licence, security review, and exit treatment.

## Intellectual property and publication

- Record background IP before work begins.
- Define foreground/project IP ownership per work package.
- Prefer interoperable standards, reproducible evaluations, and wide publication
  of results.
- Permit controlled disclosure when releasing weights, exploit details, or
  security-sensitive capabilities would create a material risk.
- Publish the reason, authority, review date, and appeal path for every
  controlled-disclosure decision.
- Never use secrecy to hide safety incidents, failed evaluations, or conflicts
  of interest from authorized oversight.

## Inspection and verification

Independent inspectors should be able to verify:

- physical and network isolation;
- model and dataset manifests;
- compute allocation and capability changes;
- evaluation integrity and contamination controls;
- audit completeness and incident handling;
- compliance with peaceful-purpose restrictions.

Inspection must protect personal data, legitimate security information, and
lawful background IP without making compliance unverifiable.

## Formation sequence

1. Publish the concept note and technical programme.
2. Recruit three to five research partners for a non-binding working group.
3. Agree shared experiments and failure-reporting rules.
4. Run a distributed replication before pooling major capital.
5. Form the consortium and independent oversight function.
6. Establish a joint facility only after governance and workload evidence exist.
7. Seek government participation after a credible scientific collaboration is
   operating, rather than presenting a treaty as the first deliverable.
