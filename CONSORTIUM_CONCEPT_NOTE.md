# Hu-Mind International Research Consortium — Concept Note

Status: invitation for a non-binding founding working group, not a treaty,
company prospectus, funding award, or claim that AGI has been achieved.

## Purpose

Hu-Mind proposes a peaceful international research collaboration to test
whether heterogeneous offline AI systems can become more general, reliable,
auditable, and controllable than monolithic systems. The programme's long-term
target is an independently evaluated candidate AGI, but its immediate work is
falsifiable architecture research with no autonomous operational authority.

The collaboration takes inspiration from shared-science facilities such as
fusion programmes and from the Antarctic Treaty's principles of peaceful use,
scientific cooperation, exchange of results, and inspection. It does not assume
that either legal model can be copied directly into AI governance.

## Founding proposition

Invite three to five universities, public laboratories, nonprofit institutes,
or aligned companies from more than one country to form a twelve-month working
group. Each founding participant contributes at least one of:

- an independently operated offline evaluation node;
- access to suitable accelerators and systems engineering;
- benchmark, robustness, security, or energy-measurement expertise;
- governance, international law, public-interest, or research-ethics expertise;
- independent replication and custody of authenticated audit checkpoints.

No contribution buys unilateral control, a private AGI declaration, or the
right to bypass safety gates.

## First-year work

1. Review and amend the peaceful-purpose and inspection charter.
2. Select two locally deployable model families and publish licence/hardware
   assessments.
3. Freeze E1 and E5 protocols comparing single models, independent dual review,
   voting, shadow probes, and redacted peer critique.
4. Run the same signed artifacts at two independent sites.
5. Publish positive, negative, inconclusive, and aborted results with journal
   checkpoints held by every partner.
6. Produce an evidence-based shared-compute design only if measured workloads
   exceed member facilities.

## Initial resource request

The founding phase seeks in-kind access rather than an immediate large facility:

- two evaluation nodes, each capable of hosting two distinct models;
- engineering time for isolated inference, measurement, and reproducibility;
- red-team and benchmark-development support;
- legal and governance workshops;
- a modest coordination and independent-evaluation budget.

Any later accelerator purchase must cite a preregistered workload, measured
bottleneck, energy/cooling assessment, procurement comparison, and rollback
plan. Vendor programmes, public compute schemes, philanthropy, and member
contributions should remain diversified so no funder controls safety findings.

## Governance before scale

The working group starts by agreeing:

- peaceful-use exclusions and prohibited applications;
- membership, voting, recusals, conflicts, and minority reports;
- background IP, project IP, publication, and defensive security review;
- model/data import, inspection, incident, shutdown, and whistleblowing rules;
- independent evaluation and criteria for rejecting capability claims;
- contribution accounting that separates resource access from safety authority.

High-capability expansion requires approval under the charter and cannot be
authorized by model consensus, one member, one government, or one funder.

## Evidence and transparency

Every architectural claim maps to H1-H6 in `PROGRAMME.md`. Plans are registered
before runs; results identify exact model manifests, datasets, configurations,
evidence packs, and artifacts. Negative or aborted results require failure
records. Each partner retains authenticated audit heads so a local operator
cannot silently rewrite or truncate the record.

Sensitive security details and personal data may be access-controlled, but a
restriction must not be used to hide outcome direction, conflicts of interest,
incidents, or deviations from a registered protocol.

## Founding decision points

Prospective partners are asked to decide whether they can support:

1. the peaceful-purpose scope;
2. independent replication and inspection;
3. publication of negative findings;
4. separation of compute contributions from unilateral control;
5. an initial twelve-month, non-binding working group;
6. development of a durable consortium or joint facility only after the first
   distributed evidence is reviewed.

The immediate outcome sought is not a new institution on paper. It is a small,
credible group able to reproduce the same offline experiment independently and
jointly decide—using published evidence—what should be built next.
